# 🚀 Advanced Metric — Training Impact (LLM-proposed) df['Training_Impact'] = df['Performance_Rating'] / (df['Professional_Development_Hours'] + 1) # Interpretation: Measures performance OUTPUT per unit training INVESTMENT # Unlike raw hours, this captures whether training is translating into results
⚠️ Circular Metric Alert — Training_Impact = Performance_Rating / (Dev_Hours + 1)

The r = +0.90 correlation between Training_Impact and Performance_Rating is mathematically guaranteed by this formula: Performance_Rating appears in the numerator. Any ratio with Performance in the numerator will correlate highly with Performance — this is algebraic, not empirical. The independently meaningful signal is the direct correlation: Dev_Hours vs Performance = r = −0.020 (near-zero, p>0.05). This confirms that raw training hours have no linear association with performance. Training_Impact remains useful as a directional efficiency index for ranking employees, but its correlation with Performance should not be cited as evidence of training effectiveness.

# 🚀 Advanced Metric — Mentorship Dependency Score df['Mentorship_Dependency'] = df['Mentor_Rating'] / (df['Performance_Rating'] + 1) # High score = employee receives strong mentoring but produces low performance output # Reveals whether mentorship is DRIVING growth or COMPENSATING for struggle
⚠️ Circular Metric Alert — Mentorship_Dependency = Mentor_Rating / (Performance_Rating + 1)

The r = −0.591 correlation with Performance_Rating is structurally guaranteed: Performance appears in the denominator, so high performance mechanically produces a low dependency score. The negative correlation is an algebraic consequence of the formula, not an independent empirical finding. Independent signal: raw Mentor_Rating vs Performance = r = +0.013 (near-zero, p>0.05) — confirming mentor quality alone has negligible linear association with employee performance. Mentorship_Dependency remains directionally useful for identifying employees heavily reliant on support relative to output, but r = −0.59 should not be cited as proof that high mentorship causes low performance.

Training_ROI uses the identical formula as Training_Impact from Q6: Performance_Rating is in the numerator. What this legitimately confirms: Very High ROI employees use 46.0 hrs vs 53.6 hrs for Low ROI — more hours do not produce more performance (r = −0.020, independent valid finding). Use the quartile hours pattern as your action signal, not the ROI↔Performance correlation.

💡 LLM-Generated Hypotheses — Why Trained Employees Underperform

H1
Engagement Deficit
Disengaged employees attend training but do not internalize or apply it. Training without motivation is passive consumption, not active learning. Low ROI employees show marginally lower engagement (80.47 vs 79.89).
H2
Job-Fit Mismatch
Generic training programs don't address individual performance gaps. When content doesn't map to actual role challenges, employees cannot transfer learning to their work context.
H3
Burnout from Overwork
Higher overtime hours in the low ROI segment indicate cognitive overload. Employees who are already overwhelmed by workload cannot absorb or apply additional training stimuli.
H4
Satisfaction Context Failure
Low job satisfaction creates an unfavorable environment that undermines learning transfer. Even effective training fails when contextual conditions don't support application on the job.
H5
Post-Training Mentorship Gap
Low ROI employees often show high Mentorship_Dependency (Q7). They need structured guidance to apply training — but receive no formal post-training support bridge between learning and execution.
H6
Career Misalignment
When training is not aligned with Career_Goals_Achievement_Status, employees lack intrinsic motivation to internalize and apply the material. Purpose-driven learning dramatically outperforms mandated training.

🗺️ ROI-Level Intervention Map

🔴 Low ROI Employees

Diagnose engagement and job-fit first. Redesign program relevance to individual role. Add 1-on-1 post-training coaching sessions before adding more training hours.

🟡 Medium ROI Employees

Increase mentorship touchpoints and implement post-training application checks. Set bi-weekly performance micro-goals tied directly to training content.

🟢 High ROI Employees

Recognize and accelerate. Connect to advanced programs and stretch assignments that challenge their efficiency further. Consider peer teaching roles.

🌟 Very High ROI Employees

Model and replicate. Extract best practices as an organizational playbook. Use them as training design consultants to improve programs for lower ROI segments.
