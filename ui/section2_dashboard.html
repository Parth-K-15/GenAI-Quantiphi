💡 LLM-Generated Hypotheses — Why Trained Employees Underperform

H1
Engagement Deficit
Disengaged employees attend training but do not internalize or apply it. Training without motivation is passive consumption, not active learning. Low ROI employees show marginally lower engagement (80.47 vs 79.89).
H2
Job-Fit Mismatch
Generic training programs don't address individual performance gaps. When content doesn't map to actual role challenges, employees cannot transfer learning to their work context.
H3
Burnout from Overwork
Higher overtime hours in the low ROI segment indicate cognitive overload. Employees who are already overwhelmed by workload cannot absorb or apply additional training stimuli.
H4
Satisfaction Context Failure
Low job satisfaction creates an unfavorable environment that undermines learning transfer. Even effective training fails when contextual conditions don't support application on the job.
H5
Post-Training Mentorship Gap
Low ROI employees often show high Mentorship_Dependency (Q7). They need structured guidance to apply training — but receive no formal post-training support bridge between learning and execution.
H6
Career Misalignment
When training is not aligned with Career_Goals_Achievement_Status, employees lack intrinsic motivation to internalize and apply the material. Purpose-driven learning dramatically outperforms mandated training.

🗺️ ROI-Level Intervention Map

🔴 Low ROI Employees

Diagnose engagement and job-fit first. Redesign program relevance to individual role. Add 1-on-1 post-training coaching sessions before adding more training hours.

🟡 Medium ROI Employees

Increase mentorship touchpoints and implement post-training application checks. Set bi-weekly performance micro-goals tied directly to training content.

🟢 High ROI Employees

Recognize and accelerate. Connect to advanced programs and stretch assignments that challenge their efficiency further. Consider peer teaching roles.

🌟 Very High ROI Employees

Model and replicate. Extract best practices as an organizational playbook. Use them as training design consultants to improve programs for lower ROI segments.
