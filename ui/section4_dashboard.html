⚠️ Calibration Paradox: Feature Importance is Unreliable at Near-Random AUC

The low-combo group (low creativity, low onboarding delay, low overtime) achieves a 57.6% success rate — higher than the high-combo group (56.0%). This inversion means the top-ranked features are not capturing causal success factors. When AUC ≈ 0.50, permutation importance rankings are noise-dominated and should be treated as hypothesis-generators, not established predictors.

✅ This model CAN be used to…
  • Rank project environments by relative risk for prioritization
  • Generate hypotheses about which environmental factors to study further
  • Identify projects sharing similar contextual characteristics
  • Justify investment in environment-focused HR programs
❌ This model CANNOT be used to…
  • Predict whether any individual project will succeed or fail
  • Justify assignment or dismissal of personnel
  • Establish which features cause success (causation unproven)
  • Replace expert project management judgment
In complex project environments, success is not a trait you can hire for; it is an environment you must build. Predicting success fails when the environment is unstable.

📋 Recommended Action Plan

  • De-emphasize predictive hiring algorithms based purely on skill scores.
  • Investigate why 'low-stress' environments yield higher success rates.
  • Use this model directionally to flag high-risk project environments, not individual failure.

⚠️ The Analyst-Complexity Cliff

At 60.2% failure rate, Analysts on Complex projects represent the single most acute project risk combination in the dataset. While Managers face a modest complexity penalty (27.4% → 34.9%), Analysts experience a structural breakdown at complexity — nearly double their failure rate on Simple projects. This demands targeted intervention.

💡 Manager Resilience at Scale

Managers are the most complexity-resilient role — their failure rate increases only 7.5 percentage points from Simple to Complex projects (27.4% → 34.9%). This suggests Managerial capabilities (coordination, risk management, stakeholder communication) provide a meaningful buffer against complexity-induced failure that Analyst and Developer capabilities do not.

🔍 Developer Complexity Immunity

Interestingly, Developers are essentially immune to complexity variation — their failure rate is nearly flat across Simple (32.1%), Moderate (35.3%), and Complex (32.9%) projects. This is consistent with technical skills being applied to well-defined sub-tasks regardless of overall project scope.

🔍 Why Univariate Tests Miss This

Both Role (p>0.37) and Complexity (p=0.62) individually appear non-significant. But their interaction reveals a 60.2% Analyst failure rate in complex projects — a real, practically important pattern. This is a classic case where multi-variable interaction analysis surfaces what univariate tests cannot.

✅ What DOES Predict Project Success — The Evidence-Based Framework

Synthesizing Q15, Q16, Q17, Q18 into actionable success drivers

🥇 #1 — Institutional Knowledge (Tenure)

Tenure_Years is the only statistically significant differentiator (p=0.020, Cohen's d=0.09). Experienced employees deliver better outcomes — not because they are "better" but because institutional knowledge reduces decision error. Implication: assign experienced staff to complex, high-stakes projects.

🥈 #2 — Onboarding Quality (Environmental)

Onboarding_Delay is the #2 predictive feature in Q17's Random Forest model. Projects with onboarding friction produce more failures. Implication: ensure new project members are fully onboarded before project kickoff, not during it.

🥉 #3 — Creative Environment (Not Trait)

Creativity_Rating is the #1 feature by importance in Q17 — but it is best interpreted as a proxy for psychological safety and work environment quality, not innate creativity. Teams that feel free to experiment produce better project outcomes.

⚠️ #4 — Overtime Burden (Risk Factor)

Overtime_Hours is the #3 predictive feature — negatively correlated with success. The Low-Combo group (low overtime) shows a paradoxically higher success rate (57.6%) than High-Combo (56.0%). Sustainable workload = better outcomes.

📌 Core Conclusion: Project success is an environment you must build, not a trait you can hire for. Skills alone explain near-zero variance. Invest in institutional retention, onboarding quality, and sustainable workloads.

📋 New Actions from Interaction Analysis

  • For Complex projects: Assign fewer Analysts and more Managers or Senior Developers as primary leads — the Analyst-Complexity interaction (60.2% failure) represents actionable risk.
  • For Analyst development: Create a "Complexity Readiness" program — before Analysts are assigned to Complex projects, ensure they receive mentoring from senior staff or a Manager co-pilot role.
  • Formalize the success formula: Tenure × Low Onboarding Delay × Sustainable Overtime = Maximum Project Success Probability. Treat these as pre-project checklist items.
  • Track role-complexity assignments in the project intake system to monitor the Analyst-Complex risk pattern going forward.
