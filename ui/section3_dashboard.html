👤 The Four Behavioral Archetypes

🚀 Adaptive Innovators (24.7%)

High adaptability/creativity, moderate teamwork. They excel in ambiguity but struggle to scale ideas. Action: Pair with high-teamwork peers to convert individual creativity into scalable team output.

🤝 Collaborative Leaders (24.6%)

Highest across all 4 dimensions. The organizational glue and top performers. Action: Fast-track for senior roles and mentorship. Use as peer coaches.

📋 Steady Contributors (20.3%)

Dependable, consistent mid-range scorers. The backbone of operations. Action: Introduce stretch assignments to activate latent leadership potential.

⚠️ At-Risk Disengaged (30.4%)

Lowest soft skills, high attrition. Symptoms of job-fit mismatches. Action: Prioritize engagement diagnosis and role reallocation before advanced training.

🚨 Structural Contradiction Archetypes

🚨 Contradiction Archetypes & Interventions

⚔️ The Forced Mediator

Thrust into conflict by toxic dynamics or stakeholders, not strength. Indicator: High overtime + low WLB. Action: Redesign team composition to reduce friction.

👑 The Dominant Resolver

High leadership drives conflict resolution top-down, creating friction. Indicator: High leadership + low teamwork. Action: Coaching on inclusive facilitation.

🔧 The Structural Outlier

Role-based conflict (e.g. customer facing) inflates cases. They work in silos. Indicator: Functional isolation. Action: Separate structural from interpersonal metrics.

🔥 The Burned-Out Resolver

Once collaborative, now depleted by sustained conflict management. Indicator: Declining engagement over tenure. Action: Immediate workload redistribution.

What the Gap Means (Correctly)
These employees are staying — but their initiative is not translating into innovation output. The risk is opportunity cost (value not created), not immediate attrition. This distinction matters for intervention design: this is an innovation problem, not a retention crisis.

🚨 The 5 Blockers to Innovation

🚧 Innovation Blockers & Business Actions

🚧 Structural Exclusion

Initiative exists, opportunity does not. Action: Create open innovation platforms (hackathons, idea portals) accessible regardless of role.

🎨 Creativity-Initiative Mismatch

High initiative but low creativity yields process improvement, not novel ideas. Action: Pair proactive doers with high-creativity ideators.

⏱️ Cognitive Overload

High overtime consumes bandwidth. Busyness is the enemy of breakthroughs. Action: Protect 10-20% unstructured innovation time.

💔 Recognition Deficit

Lower engagement scores suggest unrewarded effort extinguishes initiative. Action: Reward experimentation, even failed ones.

Section 3 — Synthesis & Conclusion
Connecting the human layer of the organization

Section 3 proves that soft skills and engagement are not intangible "HR concepts" — they are the structural mechanisms that dictate organizational output.

Whether an employee collaborates (Q11 Archetypes), burns out resolving systemic friction (Q12 Contradictions), stays with the company (Q13 Retention Risk), or contributes to the future (Q14 Innovation Gap) is entirely driven by how the organization shapes their environment.

The data shows that individual failure is often system failure in disguise. The most effective HR interventions identified here are not just coaching or training — they are structural: redesigning teams, relieving overtime, opening innovation channels, and recognizing the "Silent Contributors".
