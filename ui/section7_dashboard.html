🎯 Strategic Business Directive

The null result is not a failure to find insight — it IS the insight. Here is what leadership should do.

🚫 Stop Investing in Source Optimization
  • Cease A/B testing expensive recruitment channels against each other — the data shows no ROI
  • Stop treating agency, referral, job board, or internal hire as quality signals for candidate selection
  • Remove source-based quotas or biases from your hiring manager guidelines
✅ Redirect Budget to What Actually Matters
  • Invest in post-hire onboarding quality — Phase 4 shows Onboarding_Delay is a top project success predictor
  • Optimize for cost per hire — since all sources deliver equivalent quality, choose the cheapest at equal speed
  • Invest in internal mobility programs — internal hires show the best directional retention (47.9%)
📊 Standardize Your Recruiting Process
  • With no significant channel differences, focus on standardizing the interview and assessment process — this is likely the key quality lever not captured here
  • Implement structured interview scorecards uniformly across all sources
  • Track 90-day performance post-hire as the real quality metric going forward
🔄 Invest in Internal Mobility
  • Internal hires show the lowest resignation rate (47.9% vs 52.0% for referrals) — a directional signal worth investing in
  • Build structured internal job boards, lateral move programs, and skill-based internal matching
  • Phase 5 cross-link: WLB is the primary retention driver — prioritize role design over channel optimization

🔍 Finding: The Referral Paradox

Employee referrals — conventionally considered a "gold standard" channel — show the highest resignation rate (51.9%) and the lowest performance delta (−0.13 vs overall). This challenges conventional HR wisdom and suggests referral programs may generate strong network loyalty but not performance alignment or long-term organizational fit.

💡 The Agency Speed Paradox

For Agency hires, the Q3 time-band (moderate speed) shows the lowest resignation rate (44.5%). This suggests that neither rushing nor delaying agency hires optimizes retention. A "deliberate" pace for agency screening may outperform urgency-driven sourcing.

🔗 The Real Levers (Not Captured Here)

Given that hiring source, time, and cost explain nothing, the actual drivers of employee outcomes lie elsewhere: onboarding quality (Phase 4), work-life balance (Phase 5), compensation alignment (Phase 6), and engagement/skills (Phases 1–3). Recruiting is the starting line, not the finish line.

⚠️ Model Confidence Caveat

All three predictive models (performance, retention, satisfaction) produce negative R² values (range: −0.011 to −0.023), confirming that hiring-related variables have no usable predictive power. Model results are reported as secondary evidence only. The primary evidence is the directional null finding from ANOVA and Chi-square tests.

If where you hire from doesn't predict who stays or who succeeds, stop optimizing where — and start optimizing how you onboard, develop, and retain the people you hire.
